24-Hour Network Load Profile

Average aggregate demand across the 24 hours of the day

Grid Observation: EV drivers plug in immediately upon arriving at work between 08:00 and 10:00 AM, causing a sharp demand peak (~45-55 kWh aggregate network draw). Demand gradually tapers in the afternoon and drops significantly after 22:00.

Temporal & Seasonal Demand Patterns

Weekday vs Weekend Disparity:

Weekday energy demand is significantly higher than weekend demand (over 2.4x higher on average), reflecting commuter charging patterns at commercial and municipal locations.

Year-over-Year Growth:

Charging sessions show steady acceleration from 2011 to 2020, mirroring the exponential growth of electric vehicle registrations in California.

Lag Autocorrelation:

Previous-day same-hour demand exhibits a 57.3% feature importance gain, confirming strong 24-hour cyclicality in human charging behavior.

Operational Recommendations

Dynamic Pricing Strategy:

Implement off-peak time-of-use tariffs between 22:00 and 06:00 to encourage overnight charging and relieve the 10:00 AM grid spike.

Proactive Maintenance Scheduling:

Stations in the 'Higher Usage' cluster should undergo quarterly preventative maintenance rather than annual checks to prevent premature relay wear and GFCI ground faults.

Transformer Sizing & Storage:

Co-locating battery energy storage systems (BESS) at the top 5 stations can buffer peak hour draws without requiring expensive substation reinforcements.
